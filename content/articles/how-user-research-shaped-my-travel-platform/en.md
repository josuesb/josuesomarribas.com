<p>When building a product, it’s easy to guess what users want. However, that often leads to wasted time and effort. This is why in this article I explained how I used User-Centered Design (UCD) to create a travel platform that actually solves real problems for travelers. My goal is to help travelers discover hidden places with useful recommendations and real-time advice. To do this, I ran a survey to learn directly from travelers.</p><p>In this post, I’ll share the questions I asked to understand travelers’ needs, the insights I discovered from their answers, the features I designed to solve real problems, and what’s next for my project.</p><h2>Step 1: Asking the Right Questions</h2><p>Before designing anything, I needed to understand how people plan their trips, the problems they face when looking for travel information, and what features would improve their experience.</p><p>So, I created a survey using <a href="https://typeform.cello.so/0uvria0hyVD" target="_blank">Typeform</a> and asked these key questions:</p><p><strong>1. How often do you travel?</strong> - This helped me understand how engaged my audience is with travel.</p><p><strong>2. What type of trips do you prefer? </strong>- I gave people multiple choices, like:</p><ul><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Adventure (hiking, trekking, extreme sports)</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Relaxation (beaches, resorts, wellness retreats)</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Cultural (museums, historical sites, local traditions)</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Backpacking (budget-friendly, long-term, multiple destinations)</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Gastronomic (food tours, local cuisine experiences)</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Nature &amp; Eco-Tourism (wildlife, sustainable travel)</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Party &amp; Nightlife (festivals, clubs, social events)</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Road Trips (exploring by car, campervan, or motorcycle)</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Staycation (exploring your own city or nearby places)</p></li></ul><p>3. How do you usually plan your trips?</p><ul><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>People could choose multiple answers, including:</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Travel blogs and websites</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Recommendations from friends and family</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Social media (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook groups)</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Travel apps (Google Maps, TripAdvisor, Airbnb Experiences)</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Guidebooks and printed travel guides</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Online forums and communities (Reddit, Lonely Planet)</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Travel agencies and tour operators</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Spontaneous (no planning at all)</p></li></ul><p><strong>4. How important is finding hidden or unique spots to you? </strong>(Scale 1-10) - I wanted to see if people cared about discovering lesser-known places.</p><p><strong>5. What’s the best hidden place you’ve discovered?</strong> - This helped me find examples of unique locations travelers love.</p><p><strong>6. What do you enjoy most about traveling?</strong> - I wanted to know what makes travel exciting for them.</p><p><strong>7. What apps or platforms do you use for travel planning? </strong>- This showed me what tools people already trust and more importantly, what features people use for when planning a trip.</p><p><strong>8. What features would improve your trip planning experience? </strong>- I wanted people to tell me what they felt was missing.</p><p><strong>9. What frustrates you the most when searching for travel information?</strong> - This helped me understand the biggest pain points.</p><p><strong>10. What’s missing in current travel platforms?</strong> - This revealed what people wish existed.</p><img alt="" src="/images/framer-migrated/zkd3QZSypRYZ03EOknuTqwR7fCE.webp"><h2>Step 2: What I Learned from the Survey Results</h2><p>I collected responses and analyzed the data. Here’s what I found:</p><h3>1. Travel Frequency</h3><p>Most travelers in the survey travel a few times a year. This means they are not full-time travelers but still travel often enough to care about better planning tools.</p><img alt="" src="/images/framer-migrated/yK2bLFTzhisFe6gmUcr6efsJbc.webp"><h3>2. Popular Travel Styles</h3><ul><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Many prefer Relaxation (beaches, resorts, wellness retreats) and Cultural trips (museums, historical sites, local traditions).</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Backpacking, Gastronomic travel, and Eco-Tourism were also common choices.</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Some mix multiple styles (e.g., Adventure + Relaxation + Cultural).</p></li></ul><img alt="" src="/images/framer-migrated/RC9eH11yZacyOyhKqcYe7RvdNXw.webp"><h3>3. How People Plan Their Trips</h3><p>Most-used methods:</p><ul><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Travel apps (Google Maps, TripAdvisor, Airbnb Experiences).</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Recommendations from friends &amp; family.</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Social media (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook groups).</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Least-used method: Travel agencies and tour operators.</p></li></ul><h3>4. Importance of Hidden Places</h3><p>The average score was 7.9/10, meaning many travelers love discovering unique places. This enforces the needs of creating a travel app or website that focuses on unique experiences.</p><img alt="" src="/images/framer-migrated/9ongl6cfLNZwv8KbrlB2OEQIL3E.webp"><h3>5. Most Used Travel Apps</h3><ul><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Most common: Google Maps, Booking, Airbnb.</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Surprise mentions: Grindr 😱 (for social connections while traveling), ChatGPT (for recommendations).</p></li></ul><h3>6. Biggest Frustrations When Planning Trips</h3><ul><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Lack of up-to-date or complete information.</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Too many touristy places, not enough hidden gems.</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Security concerns when exploring lesser-known spots.</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Limited options for solo travelers.</p></li></ul><h3>7. What’s Missing in Current Travel Platforms?</h3><ul><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>More interactive and real-time travel advice.</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Better filters &amp; ways to display accommodations.</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Local contacts to help tourists in each area.</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>A platform that allows deeper conversations, not just reviews.</p></li></ul><h2>Step 3: Features Inspired by Real User Needs</h2><p>Using these insights, I designed features to solve the problems travelers face:</p><h3>1. Hidden Gems Map</h3><ul><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>A map of lesser-known places, recommended by travelers.</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Filters to find food spots, nature, culture, and adventure.</p></li></ul><h3>2. Smart Trip Planner</h3><ul><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Personalized itineraries based on budget, interests, and travel style.</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Budget tracker to help stay within spending limits.</p></li></ul><h3>3. Real-Time Travel Community</h3><ul><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Chat groups for solo travelers, local recommendations, and meetups.</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Travelers can ask locals for real-time advice.</p></li></ul><h3>4. AI-Powered Travel Assistant</h3><ul><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Instant travel questions answered with AI.</p></li><li data-preset-tag="p"><p>Translation &amp; cultural tips for smoother travel.</p></li></ul><h2>Final Thoughts</h2><p>Using User-Centered Design (UCD) helped me move from assumptions to real insights, allowing me to create a travel platform that truly solves problems for travelers. By conducting surveys, analyzing responses, and identifying key frustrations, I was able to design features that focus on hidden travel spots, real-time advice, and smart trip planning.</p><p>This process showed me that the best products come from listening to users first. Instead of guessing what people want, I built a solution based on real needs and pain points. Now, the next steps are to test the design, gather feedback, and refine the experience before launching.</p>
